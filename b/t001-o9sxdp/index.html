'the little demo that could'

by pearlriversound on Ateli.er

A few days ago, I had a really constructive conversation with the owner of a very important and highly respected label. It got me thinking about a question that I had never really asked myself before: When can we actually say that a track is finished? And who gets to decide whether a demo or a sketch is incomplete? Is that decision entirely up to the artist? And, on a broader level, how much does it make sense to frame music in purely professional and commercial terms, treating it simply as a finished product or commodity? Personally, I feel that in many cases a demo or a sketch can already be a complete artistic work. The fact that something is called a “demo” doesn’t necessarily mean that it is artistically or musically incomplete. This made me think about the whole idea of finished vs. unfinished music, and whether “finished” is really an objective quality of a piece, or rather a decision made by the artist based on their intention and creative process. Since I had never really looked into this question before, I did a little research into papers and writings on the subject. I found surprisingly little, but I came across some really interesting material, especially Charles Holbrow’s PhD research on Fluid Music, some IEEE research dealing with electronic music and the role of sketches in the compositional process, and “The Little Demo That Could,” an article written by Brian Knave for Electronic Musician in 1997. So, if any friends or contacts have other papers, books, essays, or research that explore demos, sketches, unfinished music, electronic music composition, or the question of when a piece can actually be considered “finished,” I’d be very happy to read them. I’m genuinely curious to hear different perspectives on this. Thanks!
